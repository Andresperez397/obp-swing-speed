Where does bat speed come from, and how much of it becomes exit velocity?

Predicting bat speed for hitters a model has never seen, and checking the lab's bat–ball physics against 2025 MLB data.

Andres Perez, M.S. Kinesiology (Biomechanics)Python · statsmodels · scikit-learnCode: github.com/Andresperez397/obp-swing-speed

Data

677 swings from 98 hitters in the OpenBiomechanics Project (Driveline Baseball): marker-based motion capture, about 120 swing metrics, exit velocity and HitTrax launch data, hitting off a machine at about 59 mph. The MLB comparison uses 118,793 balls in play with 2025 Statcast bat tracking.

Approach

  • Audit, then a frozen plan: 22 redundant duplicate variables and 51 invalid pitch speeds found and handled before modeling; plan committed to git first.
  • Unseen hitters: grouped CV by hitter, 10 folds × 20 repeats; leakage-tested.
  • Fair tuning: elastic net and boosted trees both tuned by grouped inner CV.
  • Physics: collision efficiency q from EV = q·vpitch + (1+q)·vbat (Nathan 2003).

Findings

  • Bat speed is a hitter trait: 86% of its variance is between hitters (ICC 0.86).
  • Size gets a quarter of the way: body and bat size explain R² 0.24 for new hitters. Pelvis and trunk metrics add +0.06, not separable from zero; arm and hand metrics add +0.20.
  • Simple beat flexible: tuned boosted trees trailed the elastic net. A leaky random split made them look best (0.78 vs 0.40).
  • Physics checks out: between hitters, +1 mph bat speed = +1.22 mph exit velocity, matching 1 + q. Within a hitter it's only +0.48.
  • Lab vs MLB: q = 0.236 in the lab and 0.209 in MLB near squared-up contact. Both are near the textbook value of about 0.2.

Limitations

The sample is 98 mostly college hitters at one facility, hitting a machine at about 59 mph, so results may not transfer directly to game swings. The lab bat's material isn't documented, and it affects collision efficiency. The MLB comparison is exploratory: different populations, pitch speeds and measurement systems. With 98 hitters, R² gains under about 0.1 aren't separable from zero.
